STARSPELL · DESIGN REVIEW · 2026-10-02 · THE FIRST-OPEN LINE

THE FIRST NIGHT

Six ways to make the first five minutes of a stranger's life in STARSPELL the reason they come back tomorrow. Every concept drops the player straight into a game, scripts the opening, hides the seam where the script lets go, and teaches the rules by doing — then each one was attacked by two critics: a cold-player advocate and a feasibility judge who verified every claim against the shipping code (v0.111.1, HEAD 8896ac8b). This page is the next rev of the first-open line — v0.75.0 THE FIRST OPEN, your 9/2 wordless ride, stays the skeleton everywhere it deserves to.

Skylar's ask, verbatim (10/2): "We need to design a tutorial for the game that will also be the first thing a new player sees when they launch the game for the first time. A very important part of this tutorial, or the start of the game for a new player, is that it really needs to hook people in the first 5 minutes of the game. That's what's going to get them to come back and keep playing the game. I need you to build a design page on a Sky Pilot GitHub page and give me ideas on how we can make the first 5 minutes of a new player's experience of the game amazing. We want to hook them into the game so that they'll continue playing while also explaining the rules of the game. This first game they play should put them right into a game, and it should be scripted so that we can guide them where we want. At some point, maybe unscripted, but we need some cool animations and whatnot. I want you to put this in the design page with multiple different ideas so I can look at it and really see what would be a great hook in those 5 minutes. We can build it into the game after I check out the design page."

How to rule: read section I (what five minutes buy today), walk the six concepts, then tap a chip on each numbered question at the bottom and COPY VERDICTS back to the console. Nothing here is built.

I · What five minutes buy today

The shipped first open is already good — and already yours: a wordless bare meadow, the wordmark condensing from stardust, an auto-rise nobody is asked to tap, a curated board where a painted hand walks S·T·A·R against a star-beast. These are real captures from this morning (headless Chrome at DPR 3, fresh profile, Firebase blocked, live v0.111.1 bytes):

Five code-verified facts that frame everything below:

  • The whole opening is silent. WebAudio cannot start before a user gesture, and the first open never asks for one — the intro, the riser and the arrival chime (the prettiest sound in the game) have been inaudible for every new player in the game's history. The first sound a stranger ever hears is their first tile tap.
  • Fight 1 is a dice roll. The board is curated; the beast is not — a uniform draw across 13 beasts spanning VULPES (30 hp, atk 8) to URSA (76 hp, atk 14). The difficulty of minutes 2–4 is a 2.5× coin flip. Every concept below pins it; all six designers arrived there independently.
  • The hand teaches two of the seven minute-one rules (weave, cast) and retires forever at the first cast. The fuse, HP, dew, refills, SCRY — all learned by being surprised.
  • At 5:00 the typical player is 20–40% through the run — standing at the first sigil pick or a minute into fight 2. The full first run takes 8–15 minutes, ends at DRACO (a real wall for a one-sigil newcomer), and only at its end does the player meet the meadow chrome, the daily chip, the first question of any kind. Every tomorrow-driver the game owns currently lives past minute eight.
  • The first game evaporates. Quick runs never checkpoint; a pocketed phone mid-run replays the whole first open onto nothing. The players most likely to churn — interrupted commuters — are structurally guaranteed to lose their progress.

II · The Sky Strikes First

no menu, no meadow, no name — a beast draws first blood, and the title card is the trophy for your first kill

The game refuses to introduce itself. Frame one is the battle zenith at speed — star streaks decelerating to stillness — and VULPES assembles already charged. It pounces before the board exists: a crimson −8 breathes at the point of contact and flies to a health bar that fades in just in time to catch it. The board rains in as the answer. The hand walks STAR; the first strike you saw coming lands at 1:10 — ambush, then prediction, the fuse taught as a two-beat story. And when the fox dies, its scattered stars curve back and condense into the gold STARSPELL wordmark — the game says its own name only because you made it. The meadow is withheld for the whole session: at run's end the descent reveals THE FIRST EARTH — a world you never knew had a floor, crickets heard for the first time, the daily chip already counting.

The player critic — 7/10
Strongest: the trophy title — a genuinely Snap-grade identity move … the single moment where a cold player feels the game is confident enough to make THEM pay for its name — and the two-strike fuse lesson. Weakest: every day-two asset (meadow, crickets, daily chip) is banked at minute 9–14 behind finishing the run; the player who leaves at minute 5 exits having seen zero return hooks. Churn: hurt-before-agency on a decompress-seeking audience; the silent pounce reads broken on muted phones; the first kill costs ~six words from a cold speller.
The feasibility critic — 7/10 · true size L
Two beats sold as reuse are the two needing carpentry: the title condense is scene-coupled to the meadow (an extraction-and-port, not verbatim), and THE FIRST EARTH cites two mutually exclusive shipped paths — the glide and the full-chrome restart — the described moment exists on neither. Battle's sky also has no velocity driver — the deceleration is new code. Everything else lands line-exact.
The grafts that fix it
  • Cheapen the first kill — VULPES at 18–20 hp so the slowest speller fells it by ~2:00.
  • Deliver THE FIRST EARTH on loss too — every first session, won or lost, ends on the meadow + the counting chip. The critic called this the single highest-leverage graft in the deck.
  • No dead taps ever — any tap advances the beat and sparks at the fingertip; it also arms audio so strike #2 is guaranteed to sound.

III · The Herald's Promise

A wordless game gains exactly one relationship. COLUMBA, THE HERALD DOVE — already complete in the bestiary, fourteen stars, cream tint, bob idle — assembles off your shoulder mid-ascent and spends the first night as a witness, not a narrator: it startles off its perch and flies to your health bar one beat before the first strike lands there (the fuse taught by body language), it taps the dew tile with its beak, and when your first beast scatters it catches one dying star and seats it, glowing, in its own chart. At graduation it sets that star into the daily chip and the ribbon names tomorrow's sky. Tomorrow the dove is standing there — and your star burns in that day's battle sky with a hero-star cross. Not a streak guilt-trip; a creature keeping its word.

Weakest: the caught star is staged at the most crowded instant of the night — gold rain, toast, scatter — the keepsake is the least legible object on screen at the one moment it must be the only thing that matters. The grafts that fix it
  • Write the kept-star flag at the catch, not at graduation — an app killed mid-run still gets the promise spoken on next open. Worth more than any animation.
  • De-crowd the catch — suppress the toast, wait out the gold rain, 400 ms time-dilation while the star seats.
  • Board-dim + one haptic tick under the warning — the only channel guaranteed to reach a heads-down reader.
  • Two rulings for you before any card: may three ribbon lines end the wordless first open, and is a wild COLUMBA charming or excluded?

IV · The Sky Leans In

every word louder than the last · the sky swears it never helped

The hook is engineered competence: every word the player casts lands bigger, hotter, louder than the last — and the sky reacts like it's watching something rare. A rigged refill queue (then a "kind bag" that fades 100→50→0% by fight 4) keeps 5–6 letter words findable while staying statistically invisible; the shipped celebration ladder pays each new length rung with a one-shot cue — a shooting star on the first 5+, the auroras swelling at 6+, a gold-rain burst at 7+. The first cast is THE WORD WRITTEN IN STARS: S·T·A·R hangs as four new stars threaded gold beside the fox, holds one breath, then collapses into the damage number. And VULPES is tuned to 33 hp so the ember minute always arrives — "strikes in 1 cast" with the beast at a sliver: race it or eat it, the core tension manufactured on day one at stakes of exactly 8 damage.

V · The Wordless Road

show the summit once · teach with a finger · never say a word

Your 9/2 philosophy taken to its limit: zero instruction copy, ever — even the hardcoded "tap letters to weave a word" line comes OFF for the first fight. The game opens at the true zenith with DRACO looming at 15% alpha, only two ember eyes lit, while the wordmark condenses in front of it — then one unbroken 2.8 s fall down the whole world column, so the player has seen exactly how high this world goes before they're handed the ground. On the bare meadow, one firefly asks: it breathes the hand's own gold push-ring, and the player's tap — anywhere — launches the ascent and arms the audio in the same gesture, so the riser and the arrival chime are heard on a true first open for the first time in the game's history. The hand gets exactly one encore (the dew tile). At first blood, a 2 s tilt-up: the eyes at the top of the column glint once. The opening shot, paid off inside three minutes.

Weakest: the summit payload is subliminal — the loop is open on the design page, not in the player's head. A 15%-alpha dragon nobody encodes can't pay off at 2:40.   • Tighten the lure ladder — ring at once, brighter pulse at 2.5 s, auto-rise at 4 s; reduced-motion keeps today's 850 ms.

The seven grafts every critic converged on

Independent designers, independent critics — the same fixes kept surfacing. These compose with ANY concept above (or with none, as a foundation card of their own):

  • 1 · Pin fight one. VULPES, every day of the week, tuned low enough that the slowest speller fells it by ~2:00. The 2.5× dice roll is the cheapest bug on this page.
  • 2 · Let the opening listen. Taps during the cinematics accelerate (never dead) — and the first tap arms the audio, so the riser and the arrival chime are finally heard. Dead taps teach "this game doesn't listen" before anything else is taught.
  • 3 · Put tomorrow inside the window. Every tomorrow-driver the game owns currently sits past minute eight. One wordless cue before 5:00 — a lit bestiary strip, a glinting fifth pip, a named next — for the commuter whose stop arrives mid-fight.
  • 4 · The run must survive a pocket. Mid-run state persists across an app kill and relaunches into the held fight. Three of six concepts' return drivers are fiction without it.
  • 5 · Feed the stalled speller. 8–12 s without a tap after the hand retires → one viable tile glints once. Wordless, invisible to anyone who doesn't need it, and it saves exactly the player about to leave.
  • 6 · Split the refusal grammar. Not-a-word crumbles to dust; too-short bounces home. The most frequent negative event in a word game's first night stops being ambiguous — still zero words.
  • 7 · Skip-lock the first ceremony only. A player's first lighting/trophy/catch cannot hard-cut before ~1.5 s — taps accelerate the stagger. Every later ceremony keeps the shipped 380 ms skip.
The console's read, for what it's worth: the strongest single build is a marriage — THE FIRST ASCENT's shape (the checkpointed ladder, the road revealed, the warm door) wearing THE SKY LEANS IN's feel (the ember minute, the rigged density, the ladder of cues), opened by THE WORDLESS ROAD's lure-tap so the first night is finally heard — with the seven grafts underneath all of it. THE SKY YOU LIT's permanent mark is the best second card: it composes with any winner and gives the bestiary a reason to exist. But that is one read; the chips below are yours.

IX · What building it would mean

  • Sizes, critic-verified: STRIKES FIRST L · HERALD full-M · LEANS IN M-heavy · WORDLESS ROAD M-with-fixes · SKY YOU LIT L (M with the scope cut) · FIRST ASCENT L. The seven grafts alone: S–M, and they de-risk every later card.
  • The harness is part of the price. tools/ftue-check.mjs (73 checks) pins today's flow beat by beat — every concept rewrites part of its first act and adds beacon states, and the full 14-suite battery re-greens on final bytes per house law. The critics sized this honestly; the cards should too.
